# Facebook companion post

Include one ready-to-copy Facebook caption with every daily report, before the English and Arabic infographics. This is a draft for the user, not permission to publish, schedule, tag accounts or send messages. Read the installed local profile for voice, brand names and the exact closing CTA. For other users, adapt to their language and preferences; do not import the original owner's account or keyword.

## Repeatable daily structure

1. Open with a fresh, conversational reflection on how working with Agentic AI let the user move several kinds of work forward that day. Lead with the human experience and then substantiate it with concrete outputs. Vary the wording across days; do not recycle a fixed slogan or promise a multiplier, saved hours or impossible continuous concurrency.
2. Add a short day-specific bridge, such as what the day started with and what it ended with, only when the chronology supports it. A day with few achievements needs a proportionate opening.
3. Present the useful achievements in an easy-to-scan emoji list. Describe the journey from idea/research through writing, design, revision, testing and delivery where evidenced. Explain unfamiliar products in one accessible sentence: what they do and why the result matters. Mention a project inspiration only when supported, without adding unrelated background.
4. Point to the infographic: for example, «الإنفوجرافيك بيلمّ اليوم ده في صورة واحدة 👇» in the owner's Egyptian Arabic voice.
5. Close with the user's configured skill-request CTA, preserving the exact keyword, destination and requested action. A request to send a message is different from a request to leave a comment. Do not substitute a generic engagement question or add a repository link when the chosen format delivers it by message.

The owner prefers informal first-person Egyptian Arabic, ordinary words such as «كتبنا وصمّمنا ونشرنا»، and familiar English product/tool names where natural. Smooth obvious typos without turning the voice into formal Arabic or corporate copy. Let the breadth of real work carry the productivity story; avoid a dense technical status report, test-count inventory or token-volume brag. Include meaningful output counts and preserve scheduled versus sent/published, as well as working delivery versus preparation.

## First announcement versus daily posts

The explanation of having created a daily-report skill, how it reviews yesterday's work, and its 4 AM schedule belongs to the first announcement only. Do not repeat that introduction in ordinary daily posts. The daily opening still reflects AI-assisted productivity. Mention the skill in the configured closing CTA. Use a launch introduction only when the user explicitly requests a first announcement; do not infer it merely because a new chat lacks earlier posts.

Do not turn one day's personal details into a recurring claim. Descriptions such as “a quiet day,” “I slept during the day,” or “compared with my life before AI” need the user's actual account; chat gaps and timestamps do not establish sleep, effort, hours worked or a personal productivity baseline.

## Attribution and factual review

Use the reconciled daily report as the factual source. Preserve user-supplied corrections and distinguish the user's wider work from work evidenced in Codex. An artifact mentioned or marketed in a Codex chat was not necessarily created there. Do not promote another assistant's build into a Codex achievement, or add its messages/tokens to Codex totals. If wider work is explicitly requested and supplied by the user, identify its provenance and scope separately.

Before delivery, compare the caption with the report and both images: date, names, counts, attribution, stage and scheduled dates must agree. Exclude credentials, contact lists and private customer details. Keep unfinished work in the written report, not in the promotional caption or images. Respect quiet days rather than inventing achievements to fill a list.
